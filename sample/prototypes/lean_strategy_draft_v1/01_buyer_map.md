# 01 — Buyer Map

Prototype request: Generate pipeline from dormant prospects through Gmail.  
Brand: Klaroha.

## Buyer segment

Dormant prospects who previously showed interest in Klaroha or in solving fragmented marketing/sales operations, but never converted into an active conversation.

They are not pure cold leads. They likely remember the pain, but either timing, trust, complexity, or unclear next step prevented movement.

## Awareness level

Problem-aware → solution-aware.

They know the symptoms:

- scattered execution across tools and people
- unclear campaign performance
- approval delays
- missed follow-up
- manual reporting
- too many marketing moving parts

They may not yet believe Klaroha is the specific operating layer that can solve it.

## Buyer type

Early majority.

They need reassurance, proof of practical implementation, and a low-risk first step. They will not respond well to a hype-heavy AI pitch or a vague “book a demo” ask.

## Blocking forces

### Anxiety

“Will this be another complicated tool to manage?”

### Habit

“We already have a CRM, agency, spreadsheet, dashboard, or manual process.”

### Low perceived likelihood

“This sounds useful, but will it actually bring order to our business?”

## Trust gap

Klaroha must prove operational clarity, not content generation.

The buyer needs to believe Klaroha can make the marketing-to-pipeline loop more visible and controllable without forcing a painful migration.

## Value-equation weakness

Perceived likelihood.

The buyer likely agrees that clarity is valuable, but doubts that a new system can actually produce it quickly enough to justify attention.

## Buyer language to reuse

- “too many moving pieces”
- “we don’t know what’s working”
- “follow-up is inconsistent”
- “approvals slow everything down”
- “we’re leaking opportunities”
- “we need a system, not another dashboard”

## Strategy implication

The GTM motion should begin with a small diagnostic offer that maps one leakage point in the prospect’s current marketing-to-pipeline flow.

Gmail is only the activation channel. The go-to-market strategy is reactivating dormant demand into diagnostic conversations and using those conversations to sharpen the next channel expansion.
