# Buyer Map — Klaroha Dormant-Prospect Gmail Pipeline

Status: shadow prototype fixture  
Source: user-provided Klaroha message architecture + `sample/klaroha_about_us.md` + Klaroha GTM deck context.  
Purpose: mandatory substrate for the lean Strategy Draft prototype.

## Buyer segment

Dormant prospects who previously showed interest in Klaroha or a clearer marketing operating system, but never converted into an active conversation.

They are likely overloaded business leaders, agency operators, or sales/marketing operators who recognize marketing execution is fragmented but have not yet trusted a new operating layer enough to act.

## Awareness level

Problem-aware → solution-aware.

They probably know the symptoms:

- scattered marketing tools
- slow approvals
- unclear campaign performance
- manual channel coordination
- inconsistent follow-up
- too much operational drag

They may not yet believe Klaroha is the specific infrastructure that solves it.

## Buyer type

Early majority.

They need proof, a clear implementation path, and reassurance that Klaroha brings order without adding another complicated tool to manage.

## Dominant blocking forces

1. Anxiety — “Will this actually bring clarity, or will it become another system to manage?”
2. Habit — “We already handle this manually / with our existing CRM / with our current specialists.”
3. Low perceived likelihood — “It sounds useful, but will my team actually adopt it and get measurable value?”

## Trust gap

Klaroha must prove that it can turn fragmented marketing and sales operations into a visible operating loop without disrupting current work.

The key proof is not “AI can write content.” The key proof is operational clarity:

- fewer dropped signals
- faster decisions
- clearer approval flow
- better visibility into what is working
- a practical next step that does not require a full migration

## Value-equation weakness

Perceived likelihood.

The dormant prospect may believe the pain is real and the outcome is valuable, but still doubt the likelihood that Klaroha can make the transition feel simple enough to start.

## Buyer language to reuse

- “too many moving pieces”
- “we need more clarity”
- “I don’t know what’s working”
- “we’re leaking opportunities”
- “everything is manual”
- “we need a system, not another dashboard”
- “we need better follow-up”

## Strategic implication

The Gmail pipeline should not sound like a cold pitch. It should behave like a reactivation path:

1. reopen the original pain,
2. show what has changed or become clearer,
3. offer a low-friction diagnostic,
4. avoid inflated claims,
5. route replies into a qualification path.
