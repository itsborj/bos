# Downstream Gmail Pipeline Brief — Klaroha Dormant Prospects

Status: shadow prototype output  
Input: `02_strategy_draft.md`  
Purpose: show whether the lean Strategy Draft is strong enough to drive downstream execution.

## Pipeline objective

Reactivate dormant Klaroha prospects and convert qualified replies into diagnostic conversations about marketing-operation leakage.

## Segment

Dormant prospects with prior Klaroha interest or prior contact, where outreach is compliant and brand Gmail is connected.

## Qualification logic

### Interested

Signal examples:

- asks what the diagnostic includes
- mentions current marketing chaos, unclear reporting, slow approvals, poor follow-up, or dropped leads
- asks for pricing or implementation path
- says they want to see how Klaroha works

Next action: send diagnostic booking link or ask one scoping question.

### Not now

Signal examples:

- asks to reconnect later
- says timing or budget is not right
- says they are focused on another priority

Next action: ask whether follow-up should be paused until a specific month or milestone.

### Wrong person

Signal examples:

- forwards to another operator
- says someone else owns marketing/sales operations

Next action: ask for the right contact or request permission to send a short context note.

### Objection

Signal examples:

- “We already have a CRM”
- “We already use an agency”
- “We do this manually”
- “AI tools have not worked for us”

Next action: answer with the operating-layer distinction and offer the leakage diagnostic.

### Compliance / unsubscribe

Signal examples:

- unsubscribe
- stop emailing
- remove me

Next action: suppress contact. No further outreach.

## Sequence structure to draft

### Email 1 — Reopen the pain

Job: reconnect around the operational problem, not around a product announcement.

Angle: “The issue usually is not more marketing activity. It is where signals, approvals, and follow-up leak.”

CTA: ask which part is least clear right now: follow-up, approvals, reporting, or content execution.

### Email 2 — Show the diagnostic

Job: reduce anxiety by making the next step concrete.

Angle: Klaroha can map one leakage point before asking for any broader implementation decision.

CTA: offer a short diagnostic conversation.

### Email 3 — Address CRM/tool objection

Job: distinguish Klaroha from replacing existing systems.

Angle: Klaroha is the operating loop around execution and decisions; it can work with existing tools rather than forcing a rip-and-replace.

CTA: ask whether they want to compare their current follow-up path against the operating-loop model.

### Email 4 — Close the loop

Job: give a graceful final touch while preserving future reactivation.

Angle: if pipeline clarity is not a priority now, reply with “later” or the biggest source of noise so Klaroha can follow up more usefully.

CTA: reply with “later,” “follow-up,” “approvals,” “reporting,” or “execution.”

## Required constraints for Gmail draft specialist

- Do not invent case studies, metrics, or ROI.
- Do not imply email will be sent automatically.
- Do not use high-pressure urgency.
- Do not claim Klaroha replaces the prospect’s CRM, agency, or sales team.
- Keep emails short enough for mobile reading.
- Preserve unsubscribe/compliance handling.
- Brand voice: clear, practical, operator-facing, non-hype.

## Why this proves the prototype

This downstream brief is specific enough for a Gmail sequence specialist to draft from it without needing a separate message architecture artifact. The buyer map is still visible in the blocking forces, CTA design, objection handling, and compliance gates.
