## Consumer Insight

**Observation:**  
The fastest path to VP is being the best individual contributor in the room. Companies promote their best doers.

**Tension:**  
Once promoted, the entire job changes. Your value is no longer what you can produce — it's what you can enable others to produce. Nobody teaches this transition. Most new VPs keep doing their old job because it's the only job they know how to do well, and doing it feels safer than admitting the new one is harder than it looks.

**Implication:**  
First-time VPs are privately failing at a job they were never trained for, while publicly performing confidence. The fear: if people find out I don't know how to lead, they'll think promoting me was a mistake.

## Concept 1 — "The Promotion Didn't Come With Instructions"

**Pattern:** P07 — Problem Reframe  
**Composite score: 86 / 100**

| Criterion | Score | Weight | Weighted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Originality | 8/10 | 25% | 20.0 |
| Strategic fit | 9/10 | 20% | 18.0 |
| Emotional response | 9/10 | 20% | 18.0 |
| Feasibility | 8/10 | 15% | 12.0 |
| Scalability | 8/10 | 10% | 8.0 |
| Simplicity | 10/10 | 10% | 10.0 |

**The idea:**  
Every new VP got a title and a team. Nobody gave them a manual for the new job. Vantage is the manual.

**Why it works:**  
It names the unspoken experience without shame. "No instructions" is matter-of-fact, not dramatic. It removes blame from the new VP — of course you don't know, nobody told you — and positions Vantage as the practical resource rather than an inspiration service. Avoids all "leadership guru" energy.

**Execution direction:**  
- Visual: Clean, minimal. A desk setup — laptop half-closed, notebook, plain ceramic mug — with something conspicuously absent. Warm neutral palette (off-white, warm grey, soft amber). No faces. The absence in the frame is the point.
- Hook: "You got the title. Nobody gave you the manual." / "Promoted to VP. Now what?"
- Copy: Matter-of-fact, peer-level. Short declarative sentences. Problem named in line one, solution offered in line three. No filler.
- CTA: "Book a 30-minute discovery call." Not "Start your leadership journey." Practical.
- Formats: Short-form vertical (story/reel backdrop), static square, email header.

## Concept 2 — "Everyone In The Room Is Waiting For You To Lead"

**Pattern:** P02 — Tension Spotting  
**Composite score: 78 / 100**

| Criterion | Score | Weight | Weighted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Originality | 7/10 | 25% | 17.5 |
| Strategic fit | 8/10 | 20% | 16.0 |
| Emotional response | 10/10 | 20% | 20.0 |
| Feasibility | 7/10 | 15% | 10.5 |
| Scalability | 6/10 | 10% | 6.0 |
| Simplicity | 8/10 | 10% | 8.0 |

**The idea:**  
The team is watching. The pressure of being observed — and not yet knowing how to perform the new role — is the specific terror of the first 90 days as VP.

**Why it works:**  
High emotional resonance. Visceral. Anyone in this situation recognises the feeling immediately.

**Why it scores lower:**  
The fear angle risks amplifying anxiety rather than resolving it. Execution is harder to keep from tipping into motivational-poster territory — directly excluded by brief constraints. Scalability is limited: the format lands once but is difficult to build a full campaign around without the tone degrading.

**Execution direction:**  
- Visual: A meeting room from the POV of the head of the table. Empty chairs. Or: a team looking expectantly at someone off-frame.
- Hook: "Everyone in the room is waiting for you to lead. What happens when you're not sure how?"
- Copy: Build tension in two lines, release with Vantage as the answer in the third.
- CTA: "You don't have to figure this out alone." → Discovery call.
- Formats: Static image, email — less suited to video.

## Concept 3 — "Still Doing Your Old Job?"

**Pattern:** P11 — Behaviour Mirror  
**Composite score: 74 / 100**

| Criterion | Score | Weight | Weighted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Originality | 6/10 | 25% | 15.0 |
| Strategic fit | 8/10 | 20% | 16.0 |
| Emotional response | 8/10 | 20% | 16.0 |
| Feasibility | 9/10 | 15% | 13.5 |
| Scalability | 7/10 | 10% | 7.0 |
| Simplicity | 6/10 | 10% | 6.0 |

**The idea:**  
Most new VPs keep doing the work they were promoted away from. It's what they're good at. It feels safe. The coaching insight: that's not your job anymore.

**Why it works:**  
Names a specific, recognisable behaviour rather than a feeling. Self-qualifying — only people who see themselves in it will respond. Well-suited to retargeting and warm audiences.

**Why it scores lower:**  
Less original — this framing exists in leadership content. Lower ceiling for emotional impact on a cold audience. Works better as a retargeting message than an acquisition message.

**Execution direction:**  
- Visual: Split — old role vs. new role. Or: someone at a desk doing detailed individual work with a "VP" nameplate visible in the foreground.
- Hook: "If you got promoted and you're still doing your old job — that's not humility. That's a trap."
- Copy: Three short punches. Name the behaviour, name the cost, name the exit.
- CTA: "Find out what the job actually is now." → Discovery call.
- Formats: Static square, carousel, email. Not primary video.

## Recommended Direction: Concept 1

Highest composite score (86). Strongest strategic fit and simplicity. "No instructions" framing is original without being clever for its own sake — it stays brand-appropriate and execution-flexible across all planned formats. Concepts 2 and 3 are available as supplementary creative for retargeting phases once the primary campaign is running.

## Discarded Directions
*(Written to BOS memory — what was considered and rejected before the three concepts above)*

**"Leadership Is Lonely at the Top"**  
Rejected: Cliché framing. Originality ceiling too low (P01 — Received Wisdom). Does not differentiate Vantage from 40 other coaching services using the same language.

**"The Playbook No One Gave You"**  
Rejected: Structurally similar to Concept 1 but weaker. "Playbook" reads as corporate jargon; "instructions" is more human and less worn. Running both would create campaign fragmentation without adding reach or distinctiveness.

**Transformation arc ("I was struggling, now I lead with confidence")**  
Rejected: Before/after narrative requires verified customer proof not available in the launch window. Risks feeling unsubstantiated. Parked — revisit in Phase 2 when testimonials are in hand.

## Creative Brief
*(Production spec for Concept 1 — this document feeds Stage 3 Primitive Generation)*

**Concept:** The Promotion Didn't Come With Instructions  
**Pattern:** P07 — Problem Reframe  
**Brand:** Vantage Coaching  
**Campaign:** VP Transition — Discovery Call Drive

---

### Visual Direction

Calm, minimal, professional. The aesthetic is a smart person's real workspace — not staged, not stock-photo glossy. Warm neutral palette: off-white backgrounds, warm grey surfaces, soft amber natural light. No faces. Objects that signal focused individual work: laptop half-closed, open notebook with real handwriting, plain ceramic coffee mug, uncapped pen. The absence in the scene is deliberate — the frame should feel like something is missing. That missing thing is clarity.

**Forbidden elements:** No motivational poster aesthetics. No conference room stock photography. No high-contrast shouted typography. No "boss moves" or "crushing it" visual language. No staged stock-photo handshakes or pointing-at-whiteboards.

---

### Copy Direction

- **Voice:** Grounded, direct, peer-level. Speaks like a trusted senior colleague who has been through it, not a coach selling transformation.
- **Hook style:** Declarative problem-naming. One sentence. No question mark on the opener.
- **Body:** Maximum 3 short lines for social. Maximum 5 for email. No filler. Each line earns its place.
- **CTA:** Practical and low-commitment. "Book a discovery call." / "Let's talk." Not aspirational language.
