The Problem

The Problem
Structural Failure

Distributed Sales Organizations Leak Qualified Demand

The typical distributed sales organization has solved the wrong problem. It has invested in brand, advertising, and lead generation. It has not solved the operating layer between market attention and sales execution.

The result is predictable leakage:

  • Serious buyers receive slow or no replies because inquiries are scattered across Messenger, WhatsApp, email, forms, and phone.
  • Sales time is wasted on low-quality leads because qualification depends on the individual agent's judgment and discipline.
  • Follow-up is inconsistent. Some agents call back in minutes. Others wait days. Most do not have a system.
  • HQ cannot standardize local execution. Every branch or office runs a slightly different playbook. Some agents are high performers. Most are average. The average stays average because there is no scalable way to transfer what the top agents do.
  • Performance is opaque. The organization knows spend. It does not know which source produced a qualified conversation, which agent converted it, or which offer closed it.

This is not a small-business problem. It is a structural problem in every distributed sales organization that operates with local agents, branches, franchisees, or dealers in high-value categories.

The Problem
High-Value Categories

The Categories Where This Matters Most

High-value categories share a common pattern: one qualified conversation can produce thousands of dollars in revenue, yet the system between market attention and that conversation is broken.

  • Real estate brokerages and developer sales teams. A typical developer sales team for a mid-market project may receive hundreds of inquiries per month. Only a fraction are qualified. Response speed, consistent qualification scripts, and professional follow-up determine which developer closes the most sales — not advertising spend alone.
  • Mortgage and lending networks. Distributed loan agent networks suffer the same problem. Borrower intent is high-signal but time-sensitive. The agent who responds with the right message in the first fifteen minutes is far more likely to convert than the one who calls back the next day.
  • Franchise and dealer networks. Multi-location consumer service brands, vehicle dealer groups, and equipment dealer networks all face the same HQ-versus-local tension: the brand is standardized; the sales execution is not.
  • Commercial insurance brokers. Distributed broker networks with multiple specialists in different lines of coverage. A prospect's inquiry may touch multiple specialists. Routing, qualification, and follow-up are almost entirely manual.
  • Professional service networks. Accounting, legal, and specialist consulting practices with multiple offices or partner networks face identical problems: inquiry capture is fragmented, qualification depends on the intake coordinator, and no one measures which referral source or channel produces the best clients.
The Problem
Existing Tools

Why Existing Tools Don't Solve It

The tools that exist today solve pieces of the problem without solving the operating layer.

  • CRMs (Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho) are record-keeping systems, not demand-capture systems. They require agents to enter data. Most agents don't. They also require someone to build the qualification logic, routing rules, and follow-up sequences — which is the expertise that distributed organizations don't have.
  • Marketing automation platforms (Klaviyo, ActiveCampaign, Mailchimp) are audience-broadcast tools. They are not designed for real-time inquiry qualification or branch routing.
  • Messaging platforms (Podium, Intercom, Tidio) handle conversation, not qualification and routing. They do not learn which offers produce qualified conversations or standardize scripts across agents.
  • Agency-run lead generation (outsourced ads and landing pages) produces inquiries but provides no operating layer. The agency delivers leads; the organization still has the same execution problem on the other side.
  • GoHighLevel and similar platforms serve marketing agencies, not the distributed organizations themselves. The resulting product is a white-labeled tool stack that an agency configures and manages — not an operating system the organization can run.
The Problem

Which channels are producing qualified conversations, which agents are converting them, and what should every agent do differently?

None of the tools that exist today answer the fundamental question a distributed sales leader needs answered.

The Solution

The Solution
What Klaroha Is

Demand-Capture Infrastructure, Not Another Tool

Klaroha is not a CRM, not a marketing automation platform, and not an agency service. It is the operating layer between market attention and sales execution.

The core system handles six operating functions:

01 — Intent Capture
Pages, funnels, forms, and inquiry assets that capture demand from every channel the organization uses — Google, Meta, WhatsApp, Messenger, direct, referral, listings, and partner networks — into a single qualified pipeline.
02 — Qualification Flows
Mechanisms that determine buyer seriousness before sales time is spent. Category-specific: a real estate inquiry qualification flow asks different questions than a mortgage inquiry flow or a commercial insurance inquiry flow. Klaroha comes installed with vertical playbooks.
03 — Conversation Routing
The operational transition from qualified inquiry to the right agent, branch, or specialist. Routing rules are configurable by product, geography, capacity, or business rules. Speed-to-lead is monitored.
04 — Follow-Up Engine
Automated and assisted follow-up that prevents interest from going cold. Scripts, reminders, nurture sequences, and reactivation workflows tailored to the organization's category and offer.
05 — Performance Visibility
Inquiry source, qualification rate, response time, routing outcome, agent performance, branch performance, and conversion readiness — all in one operating view. HQ sees the full picture; managers see their team; agents see their pipeline.
06 — Vertical Playbooks
Category-specific templates, scripts, qualification frameworks, and benchmarks installed at setup. The system does not start blank. A real estate brokerage onboards to a system that already understands inquiry flow, buyer qualification, and agent performance in their category.
The Solution
Product Experience

The CMO-in-Pocket Model

The product is delivered through a CMO-facing command interface backed by an AI operating system internally called BOS (Business Operating System). The AI CMO holds the organization's brand, product, audience, and operating context. It can be directed through natural language to produce campaign plans, draft assets, run qualification analyses, identify performance issues, and recommend next actions.

The owner or sales director interacts with the system the way they would interact with a senior CMO: by giving intent, not by operating tools.

The CMO understands the business. It delegates execution to specialist modules. It produces artifacts — plans, drafts, qualification flows, performance reports — for human approval. It learns from what the organization approves and rejects.

The alternative — hiring a CMO, a CRM administrator, a paid ads specialist, a follow-up automation specialist, and a performance analyst — costs $300,000+ per year for a distributed organization with multiple locations. Klaroha delivers that operating capacity at a fraction of the cost, with the additional advantage that the system is consistent across every branch.

The Solution
Defensibility

What Makes Klaroha Defensible

  • Vertical intelligence at setup. Generic tools require months of configuration before they reflect the organization's category. Klaroha onboards with vertical-specific playbooks, qualification frameworks, and performance benchmarks already installed. The organization's CMO is ready to operate from day one.
  • Multi-brand, multi-market architecture. The system is designed for operators managing multiple brands, clients, or markets from a single interface. An agency managing ten developer clients, a network managing forty franchisee locations, or a brokerage managing multiple specialist teams can all operate from one command center with clean separation of brand context and performance data.
  • LLM-driven capability manifest. Klaroha's operating system is extensible. Channel capabilities (Gmail, Google Ads, Meta, Search Console) are installed as plugins. Adding a new channel or specialist capability does not change the core operating contract — it adds a new specialist with its own skill and operating rules. This design means the platform scales its capabilities without becoming architecturally complex for the operator.
  • BITE pipeline architecture. The internal execution pipeline (Bridge–Intent–Transform–Evaluate) ensures that every marketing handoff is tracked, every AI execution is reversible with an audit trail, and no approved action happens without an explicit human decision. This is critical infrastructure for distributed organizations where compliance, brand consistency, and audit requirements are real.
  • Approval-gated execution. No AI module executes a campaign, sends a message, or changes an ad budget without human approval. The organization retains control; the system provides execution capacity. This is not a limitation — it is the design principle that makes the system trustworthy to an organization running multiple agents, branches, or client accounts.

A. Technical Architecture Overview

FastAPI + PostgreSQL + React/Vite on Self-Hosted VPS

Klaroha runs on a FastAPI + PostgreSQL backend with a React/Vite frontend deployed via Docker on a self-hosted or managed VPS. The architecture is designed for:

  • Multi-brand operation: One deployment supports multiple brands or client accounts with clean data isolation.
  • Plugin extensibility: Channel capabilities (Gmail, Google Ads, Meta, Search Console) are installed as independent plugins. Adding a channel does not require changes to the core operating system.
  • LLM-agnostic execution: The CMO can be backed by any LLM (OpenAI, Anthropic, OpenRouter). Individual specialist modules can use different models and providers optimized for their task.
  • BYOK or platform-mode AI: Organizations can use Klaroha's pooled AI access (platform mode) or register their own API keys (BYOK) for full cost control.
  • Audit trail and approval gates: Every AI execution is logged, every approved action has an explicit approval event, and no spend or publish action executes without human decision.
